Method

Clear enough to use.

The service layer should feel like editorial guidance attached to the shop, not like a separate consultancy website.

01

Start with the context.

What the room is used for, what already exists there, and what currently feels unresolved.

02

Then the object.

Material, proportion, color and placement are considered through the space rather than as isolated product claims.

03

Write it plainly.

The answer should be practical, specific and proportionate to the question instead of sounding ceremonial.

04

Leave room to decide.

The service should clarify a choice, not manufacture pressure to buy a product or purchase another service.

Service request

Send a quiet brief.

In production, this visual block should map onto the existing WordPress contact or inquiry owner. It must not invent a separate booking engine, quote system or payment workflow.

A good request can be short: the object or room, what feels unclear, and what kind of decision you are trying to make.

This prototype does not submit requests, create appointments, collect payment or generate quotes.

What this is not.

Service boundaries

No miracle claims

Services should not promise wealth, healing, destiny changes or guaranteed outcomes.

No hidden purchase

A request should not silently create an order, product, payment, subscription or booking.

No project drift
