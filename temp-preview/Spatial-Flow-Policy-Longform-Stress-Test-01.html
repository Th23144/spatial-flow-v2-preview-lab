Not production legal copy
01 · Overview

A practical path when something is not right.

This sample is deliberately written at a realistic policy length so the page can be judged under sustained reading conditions. The exact rules, dates, fees and legal language shown here are illustrative only. In production, Spatial Flow should preserve the approved policy text and operational rules already used by the business.

A return or refund page usually needs to answer several questions without forcing the customer to search across multiple support pages: whether the item can be returned, how long the customer has to act, what condition the item must be in, who pays for shipping, how refunds are issued, what happens when an item arrives damaged, and how international orders are handled.

The page therefore needs more than attractive headings. It needs a structure that remains calm after several thousand words, keeps important exceptions visible, and allows customers to find a specific clause quickly.

Stress-test rule: the visual system should remain readable even when the page becomes repetitive, procedural and text-heavy.

02 · Eligibility

What may be returned.

For layout testing, assume that standard, unused merchandise may be eligible for return when it is sent back in a condition that allows the item to be inspected and reasonably resold. Eligibility can depend on the product category, whether the item was customized, whether it was sold as final sale, and whether the customer followed the required return process.

Products should normally be reviewed against the condition in which they were delivered. Packaging, tags, inserts, certificates, accessories and any protective materials may all matter when the business determines whether a return is complete.

Typical eligibility checks

  • The item was purchased directly from the Spatial Flow store rather than from a third-party marketplace or reseller.
  • The return request was submitted within the applicable return window.
  • The item is not excluded by a product-specific or final-sale rule.
  • The item has not been materially altered, resized, damaged, worn beyond reasonable inspection, or exposed to conditions that prevent safe resale.
  • The customer can provide enough order information for the purchase to be located and verified.

Where a product page includes a more specific return restriction, the production policy should explain how that product-specific notice relates to the general policy and which rule controls if the two differ.

03 · Return window

Time should be stated plainly.

A real policy normally distinguishes between the date a return request must be opened and the date the parcel must actually be handed to the carrier. Those are different events and should not be blurred together. The page should also explain which date starts the clock: order date, shipment date, delivery date, or another event.

Customers may need additional guidance when delivery is delayed, a parcel is marked delivered before it is received, or multiple items from one order arrive in separate packages. If the policy gives the business discretion to extend a deadline, that discretion should be described clearly rather than hidden in a footnote.

Long-form clause example

For this visual test, imagine a policy that allows a customer to request a return within a stated number of calendar days after confirmed delivery, requires the parcel to be tendered to the carrier within a second stated period after authorization, and explains that unapproved parcels or parcels mailed after the authorization expires may be refused or processed under a different rule.

04 · Item condition

Inspection without turning the page into a wall of warnings.

Condition language is often one of the densest parts of a returns policy. Jewelry, stones, textiles, decorative objects and other materials can show handling in different ways, so the production policy may need both a general rule and category-specific examples.

Reasonable inspection should be separated from use. Opening a package and checking size, finish, color or fit is not the same thing as wearing or using an item for an extended period. The policy should make that distinction in language that a customer can understand before deciding whether to return something.

Examples of condition factors

  • Visible wear, scratches, chips, stains, fragrance, residue or other changes not present when the item was delivered.
  • Missing packaging, cards, pouches, accessories, certificates or components included with the original shipment.
  • Alterations, repairs, restringing, resizing or other modifications performed after delivery.
  • Damage caused by storage, water, chemicals, heat, impact, misuse or failure to follow product-care instructions.
!

A visually quiet policy still needs strong hierarchy for clauses that materially change a customer’s eligibility.

05 · Exceptions

Exceptions need enough space to be understood.

Some categories may be excluded from ordinary returns for hygiene, customization, made-to-order production, final-sale pricing, perishability or other operational reasons. A robust template must be able to hold several exception categories without visually collapsing into a dense legal block.

Illustrative non-returnable categories

  • Customized, engraved, resized or otherwise personalized pieces.
  • Items clearly marked final sale before checkout.
  • Gift cards or other stored-value products where applicable.
  • Products that cannot safely be resold after use for hygiene or contamination reasons.
  • Items damaged after delivery through misuse, improper care or unauthorized modification.

If an excluded item arrives damaged, defective or different from what was ordered, the policy should separately explain the remedy. “Non-returnable” should not be used as a blanket answer to an order-fulfillment error.

06 · Return process

A process can be detailed without feeling bureaucratic.

The return process should tell the customer what to send, where to send it, and what information should be included. If authorization is required, the policy should say whether the customer must wait for approval before shipping the parcel.

  1. Locate the order number and the email address used at checkout.
  2. Contact support or use the designated return-request route and identify the item being returned.
  3. Provide the reason for the request and, when relevant, photographs showing damage, defects or an incorrect item.
  4. Wait for return instructions when authorization is required.
  5. Pack the product securely with the required accessories and documentation.
  6. Use the instructed carrier or shipping method when the business provides a label.
  7. Keep the shipment receipt and tracking information until the return is complete.

Unapproved returns

A longer production policy may need a separate clause for parcels sent without authorization, returns mailed to the wrong address, cash-on-delivery shipments, or packages that create unexpected customs or handling charges. This section tests how secondary operational detail sits beneath the main process without overpowering it.

07 · Shipping costs

Who pays, and under which circumstances.

Return-shipping responsibility may vary by reason. A customer-choice return can be treated differently from a damaged item, a fulfillment error, or a package that arrived materially different from the order. The production policy should state those distinctions directly.

Original outbound shipping, expedited-service fees, duties, taxes and return-shipping fees may each follow a different refund rule. If any of those amounts are non-refundable, that should be stated before the customer ships the item back.

Packaging responsibility

The policy may also require reasonable packaging. If a returned item is damaged in transit because it was inadequately packed, the business may need a rule describing how that affects the refund. This kind of clause is intentionally included here because it creates the repetitive, detail-heavy reading pattern that a short mockup cannot reveal.

08 · Refund timing

Dense timing information, still scannable.

A useful refund section distinguishes receipt of the return, inspection, approval, initiation of the refund, and the separate time the customer’s bank or card issuer may take to post the funds. Those events should not be represented as one vague “processing time.”

09 · Damage / errors

Problems on arrival need a different route.

If an item arrives damaged, defective, incomplete or materially different from what was ordered, the customer should have a clear support path that does not require interpreting the ordinary change-of-mind return rules.

Information that may help resolve the issue

  • Order number and the email used at checkout.
  • Photographs of the item, packaging and shipping label where relevant.
  • A short description of what is damaged, missing or incorrect.
  • Whether the outer parcel showed visible damage at delivery.

The production policy should also explain whether the customer must retain packaging during the claim process, whether the item should be returned before a replacement is sent, and what happens if replacement stock is unavailable.

10 · International orders

Long policies become harder when borders are involved.

International returns can involve duties, import taxes, carrier brokerage, customs declarations, longer transit times and country-specific restrictions. The visual template must therefore tolerate a section that is longer and more procedural than the rest of the page.

Where duties or taxes were paid to a government, carrier or third-party service, the store may not be able to refund those amounts directly. A production policy should say whether the customer must seek reimbursement from the relevant authority and whether the store can provide supporting documents.

Customers should also be told how to declare the parcel for return shipment, whether prepaid labels are available in every country, and what happens if a parcel is refused, abandoned or returned because customs charges were not paid.

11 · Contact

Finish with one clear next step.

If the customer is unsure whether an item is eligible, cannot locate the order, received the wrong product, or needs help with a return already in transit, the page should end with a direct support route rather than another promotional section.

For a production implementation, the contact details, support hours, response expectations and links must come from the real WordPress content or approved business policy rather than this stress-test sample.

Have the order number and checkout email ready when the question relates to an existing purchase.
