## User validation

The user fully validates the current Future Commerce Thesis, including the restraint principle:
depth should be available, not mandatory.

## User communication concern

The internal principle:
"an object entering the online world should remain an object"

is conceptually correct and understandable internally, but too compressed for external strategic communication.
If used alone in conversation with partners, investors, collaborators, or future team members, it lacks explanatory force.

Therefore About planning must maintain two parallel language layers:

1. **Internal strategic shorthand**
   - compact concepts such as "object-centric commerce" and "an object should remain an object";
   - useful for design/product decisions.

2. **External explanatory language**
   - explains the structural problem, why it matters, and what changes in practice;
   - persuasive without becoming slogan-heavy or abstract.

## Working external explanation

The deeper idea is:

When culturally, materially, or craft-rich physical goods move online, conventional ecommerce often strips away much of what makes them meaningful:
material variation, provenance, craftsmanship, cultural context, relationships, use, and individuality.

Spatial Flow's thesis is not to reject ecommerce efficiency, but to preserve more of the object's real-world richness while still making discovery and purchase efficient.

A stronger explanatory sequence should be:
**real object → information loss in standard ecommerce → why that loss matters → Spatial Flow's response.**
