# About Us Planning — Ecosystem Positioning Correction and DIY Wedge

Date: 2026-09-26
Project: Spatial Flow V2 / Project 2

## User correction

The prior V0 framing over-isolated Spatial Flow as a standalone commerce brand.

The user clarifies:
- Spatial Flow is not intended to be a pure commerce site;
- Ink & East is evolving far beyond a blog into a larger platform;
- Spatial Flow may remain related to that platform and can plausibly function as a commercial / practice-facing partner within the wider ecosystem;
- therefore the About strategy should not create a worldview that materially diverges from Ink & East.

## Strategic correction

The About plan should distinguish four layers:

1. **Worldview / ecosystem layer**
   - the larger intellectual and cultural territory shared with Ink & East;
   - Spatial Flow should not invent an incompatible ideology merely to sound like an independent ecommerce brand.

2. **Spatial Flow role layer**
   - what Spatial Flow specifically contributes within that broader territory;
   - commerce, product experience, customisation, services, and practical interaction with objects / spaces can sit here.

3. **Customer problem layer**
   - concrete frictions in buying and understanding products online.

4. **Product / moat layer**
   - features that materially improve the experience and can prove the brand's practical value.

## DIY / 360° insight

The user identifies a concrete ecommerce friction:
online shopping cannot reproduce direct physical inspection. Product imagery is often selected, retouched, lit, and color-managed; therefore perceived color, texture, scale, and detail can differ from reality.

The proposed Custom Studio / DIY system is intended to reduce that gap through:
- real photographed material assets;
- 360° / multi-angle inspection;
- direct assembly / preview;
- a more transparent relationship between the digital preview and the physical object.

## Professional judgment

This is strategically strong, but it should currently be treated as:
- a **high-value product wedge / proof point**;
- a concrete answer to "why buy / interact here instead of a generic product grid";
- potentially part of the answer to "why Spatial Flow deserves to exist".

It is NOT yet sufficient, by itself, to define the entire existential reason for Spatial Flow.

Reasons:
- it solves a major commerce-experience problem, but not the whole brand/platform role;
- real photography and 360° preview reduce uncertainty but cannot mathematically eliminate display, lighting, material, and device color differences;
- "market-disruptive / dimensionality-reduction attack" should remain an internal ambition unless validated against actual competitor capability and user outcomes.

## Next planning task

Do not ask the user to produce a polished mission statement.

Instead decompose "Why does Spatial Flow exist?" into:
A. What broader territory does Ink & East / Spatial Flow believe is underserved?
B. What specific job should Spatial Flow perform inside that territory?
C. What concrete user frictions can Spatial Flow solve better than ordinary commerce sites?
D. Which current or planned product systems prove that claim?

Current strongest answer to C/D:
the Custom Studio / real-shot / 360° preview system reduces the online-vs-physical inspection gap and makes customization more inspectable and trustworthy.
