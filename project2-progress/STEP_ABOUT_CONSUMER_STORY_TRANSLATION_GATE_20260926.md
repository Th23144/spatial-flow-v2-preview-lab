## Critical next phase

Move from founder / strategy language into **consumer-facing brand narrative**.

The problem to solve is not:
"How do we explain every strategic idea?"

It is:
"Which strategic truths should a normal visitor actually feel, understand, and remember after reading About Us?"

## Hard rule

Future About must clearly separate:
- **What Spatial Flow is today**
- **What Spatial Flow believes**
- **What Spatial Flow is building toward**

Do not present future vision as current capability.

## Consumer translation principles

1. Strategy stays backstage; meaning moves frontstage.
2. Avoid investor language: moat, flywheel, right-to-win, object graph, SKU-centric, defensibility.
3. Preserve the underlying truth behind those ideas in plain, credible language.
4. Do not overload the user with every capability or future plan.
5. About must create a coherent emotional and intellectual progression, not a strategic memo.
6. Current proof should be concrete; future ambition should be framed honestly.
7. Spatial Flow must remain distinct from Ink & East; any relationship should be explained only at the level actually intended for public communication.

## Immediate planning task

Define the consumer narrative spine:
- what opens the story;
- what problem / tension is introduced;
- how Spatial Flow's point of view is revealed;
- what current proof is shown;
- what future ambition is hinted at;
- how the page resolves into next actions.
