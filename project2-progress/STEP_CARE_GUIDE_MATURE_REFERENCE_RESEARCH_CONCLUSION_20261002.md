## Cross-reference finding

Mature care systems usually do NOT make the care page carry the brand's full philosophy.

The common structure is:
1. short brand-toned introduction;
2. immediate route to the relevant material / product / category;
3. concise actionable care guidance;
4. repair / professional service / support when relevant;
5. detailed care pushed down to material- or product-specific pages when necessary.

Brand character comes mainly from:
- typography / imagery / spacing;
- material language;
- concise warm framing;
- confidence and restraint.

It does NOT usually come from repeated manifesto statements, lifecycle diagrams, chapter theatre, or multiple overlapping hierarchy systems.

## Reference-specific lessons

### Tiffany
- landing page stays simple and category-led;
- detailed care is separated by material;
- services are a clear downstream path;
- brand tone is present but utility dominates.

### Mejuri
- material-first taxonomy is extremely legible;
- one short warm line is enough to make the page feel human;
- users can immediately choose Metals / Stones / Other Materials;
- care services appear after the reference content.

### ferm LIVING
- strongest reference for Spatial Flow's broader future category mix;
- permits a small amount of ownership / longevity philosophy in the intro;
- immediately transitions into General Care and material-specific instructions;
- care identity comes from material awareness, not brand-manifesto language.

### Hermès
- strongest reference for premium aftercare tone;
- uses material-specific language such as leather evolving / patinating over time;
- repair and craftsmanship extend ownership;
- however the language remains tied to real material behavior and real services.

### HAY / Muuto
- useful for taxonomy discipline;
- general guidance + material-specific maintenance;
- care is treated as product information infrastructure, not editorial spectacle.

### Louis Vuitton
- detailed product care often lives on the specific product page;
- reinforces that a global Care Guide should not try to contain every product-specific rule.

## Diagnosis of Spatial Flow's current "strange" feeling

The current Care Guide is trying to perform too many roles at once:
- brand expression;
- practical care reference;
- material education;
- placement / spatial guidance;
- ownership philosophy;
- support routing.

Refinement 02 intensified the mismatch by adding explicit strategic language such as ownership-after-checkout and a lifecycle strip.

The mature-reference pattern suggests:
- reduce the global page's conceptual burden;
- treat it as a premium care gateway / practical reference;
- use one restrained brand-toned intro;
- surface the care index immediately;
- prioritize action + material / object grouping;
- demote Placement Notes to a secondary section so it does not redefine the whole page;
- keep support at the end;
- leave product-specific care detail to product-level content where appropriate.

## Recommended Spatial Flow direction

Do not redesign from scratch yet.

Next candidate should:
- keep the stronger Wishlist-led visual system;
- remove the explicit lifecycle manifesto from Refinement 02;
- simplify hierarchy;
- make Care Index the real functional anchor;
- let material / handling language create the care feeling;
- keep Placement, but visually secondary;
- use no more than one short ownership / longevity statement;
- retain WordPress editability and current factual content boundaries.
