# About Us Macro Planning — ACT I: Before It Becomes a Product

Date: 2026-10-01
Project: Spatial Flow V2 / Project 2

## Status of title

"Before It Becomes a Product" is retained as an internal working title only.
It is NOT yet approved as final user-facing copy.

## Why ACT I exists

ACT I should establish the first principle behind the entire About narrative:

A physical object contains more than the commercial representation used to sell it.

For materially, culturally, or craft-rich objects, important qualities may include:
- material variation;
- texture;
- light response;
- scale;
- individual differences;
- craftsmanship;
- provenance / origin;
- use context;
- relationships with people, other objects, and space.

The point is not to romanticize physical objects or reject ecommerce.
The point is to establish that standard digital commerce necessarily compresses reality, and that some categories lose more than others in that compression.

## What the user should understand after ACT I

The visitor should leave ACT I with a simple recognition:

**some objects lose important parts of themselves when they are reduced to a conventional product representation.**

The visitor does NOT yet need to know the full Spatial Flow solution.

ACT I creates the tension that makes ACT II meaningful.

## What the user should feel

Primary:
- curiosity;
- recognition;
- a sense that the brand is paying unusually close attention to the object itself.

Secondary:
- mild tension / incompleteness;
- desire to understand what Spatial Flow intends to do differently.

Avoid:
- guilt;
- anti-commerce rhetoric;
- academic heaviness;
- mystical vagueness;
- the feeling that the brand is lecturing the visitor.

## What belongs in ACT I

At macro level:
- the real object as the starting point;
- material / physical specificity;
- the idea of information loss in digital representation;
- the contrast between object richness and commercial simplification;
- the principle that efficiency and standardization are useful but incomplete for certain objects.

## What does NOT belong in ACT I

Do not place here:
- a detailed company introduction;
- the full "why Spatial Flow exists" explanation;
- current Shop / Studio / Services feature proof;
- Ink & East;
- future product categories;
- moat / right-to-win / Object Graph language;
- a long critique of the ecommerce industry;
- final mission statement;
- detailed feature demonstrations.

## Strategic classification

ACT I is primarily:
- **BRAND BELIEF**
supported by:
- a broadly observable product / commerce tension.

It should not claim unproven current capabilities.

## Relationship to ACT II

ACT I should end with an unresolved but intelligible question:

If some materially / culturally rich objects lose too much when treated as ordinary ecommerce inventory, **what would it look like to handle them differently?**

ACT II then introduces Spatial Flow as the present-day answer-in-progress.

## Critical restraint

ACT I must not become a long artistic prologue that delays brand clarity.

The opening can be expressive, but Spatial Flow must become explicit by the next major narrative transition.

## Macro decision

ACT I's job is NOT:
"show how beautiful objects are."

Its job is:
**make the visitor understand why the way an object is represented matters.**
