Section · 01 The piece, slowly. Material · Smoky quartz
Edition · 9 of one
Made · Spring, 2026
Studio · Mori, Okayama

Smoky quartz is, in a sense, the patient stone. It does not draw attention to itself. It absorbs — light, attention, the small static of a difficult morning — and it holds. This particular strand was cut in March from a single block Hayato had been keeping under his bench for three years, waiting for the right form to ask for it.

Each of the twelve beads has been individually shaped, then tumbled for nine days in river water mixed with fine grit. The surface is matte, almost dry to the touch — closer to a polished stone you'd find on a Welsh beach than to the glassy quartz you might know from a jewellery counter. We asked Hayato not to seal it. He preferred not to anyway.

山有靜氣，石承之。 The mountain holds a quiet air; the stone receives it.

The strand is held on a 1.2 mm waxed Belgian linen cord, knotted between every two beads in the old way — so if it ever breaks (which it will, in years, with use), only one or two beads can travel before the next knot stops them. Send it back to us when that day comes; we'll restring it for the cost of the cord.

This piece is the seventh of an edition of nine. Numbers are stamped into a small pewter disc at the clasp; ours reads VII / IX.

The story behind
the strand.

Hayato Mori · Okayama, JP
Hayato Mori
Stonecutter, fourth generation · Okayama, Japan

Hayato grew up in a town built on Bizen pottery, but his grandfather worked stone — tomb markers, mostly, for the small temples that line the prefecture. When the temples stopped commissioning, the workshop was left to Hayato and a small set of carbide chisels.

He works alone, in a converted rice store overlooking a bend in the Asahi river. He cuts no more than thirty-six pieces a year, in three editions of twelve, and he refuses to take orders out of season. We have worked with him since 2022; this is our fourth strand.

If you'd like to read the long form — how we found him, the year he stopped working entirely, what he carries in his apron pocket — we've published the studio visit in our journal.

Section · 03

Care, and a little ritual.

i.

The everyday.

Wear it. Smoky quartz darkens, very slightly, with skin oils — this is welcome. If it feels dusty, breathe on it and wipe it with a dry cotton cloth. Avoid scented oils on the cord; the linen will hold the smell.

Don't sleep with it on. The stone wakes earlier than you do.

ii.

The seasonal.

Once a season — on the first cold morning, the first warm one — rinse the strand in cool, unsoaped water and let it dry on a stone or wooden surface, never in direct sun.

If you keep an altar, or a still corner, leave it there overnight. A windowsill catching the moon is enough.

iii.

The repair.

The cord is meant to wear; the stone is not. When the linen begins to soften or fray (typically after two to three years of daily wear), send the strand back to our Lisbon studio in the original cloth pouch.

We restring it in our hand. Cost of cord only; no labour. This is a promise.
