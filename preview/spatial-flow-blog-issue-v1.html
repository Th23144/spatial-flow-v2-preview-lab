Issue No. 001 · Vol I · Spring 2026

Wu Wei is not laziness.

無為，非懶

An issue on the unease of doing nothing — read through Laozi, Zhuangzi, and the long tradition of Chinese thinkers who treated rest not as failure, but as a kind of intelligence.

8 pieces · ≈ 40 min total · Published 2026.04.20
無
This issue's character · Wú · not-forcing
墨東
Editor's Note 編 者 按

This issue began with a sentence I did not write. A reader, in a letter we received last winter, asked: "Why do I feel guilty when I rest?" I read the letter four times. I made tea. I read it again. Around it, slowly, an issue began to form.

The Chinese tradition has a long argument with this question. Not a sermon — an argument. Laozi said the empty space inside the bowl is what makes the bowl useful. Zhuangzi said the cook who never sharpens his blade is the cook who has stopped fighting the meat. We have, in eight pieces, tried to listen to that argument plainly, in English, without dressing it up.

You will not find advice here. You will find ways of seeing. That is, I think, what an old book is for.

— The Editors · Spring 2026 · Beijing
